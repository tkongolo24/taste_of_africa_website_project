Injera

Injera is the sour, spongy flatbread at the center of Ethiopian and Eritrean meals. It is made from teff, a tiny ancient grain, fermented for days. Diners tear off pieces to scoop up stews such as doro wat and lentil misir.

Ingredients

Servings: 4
  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 2.5 cups water, plus extra
  • 0.5 tsp salt
  • 0.25 tsp dry yeast (optional starter boost)

Instructions

Step 1: Ferment the batter
  1. Whisk the teff flour, water, and yeast into a smooth batter.
  2. Cover loosely and leave at room temperature for 2 to 3 days until bubbly and pleasantly sour.
Step 2: Prepare to cook
  1. Stir in the salt and enough water to make a thin, pourable batter like crepe batter.
Step 3: Cook the injera
  1. Heat a non-stick pan, pour in the batter in a spiral from the edge to the center.
  2. Cover and cook 2 to 3 minutes until the surface is full of small holes. Do not flip.
