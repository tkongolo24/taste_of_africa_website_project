Injera

Injera is a sour, spongy flatbread central to Ethiopian and Eritrean cuisine. It is traditionally made using teff, a tiny ancient grain, and fermented to develop its distinctive flavour and texture. Injera is commonly served with traditional stews such as doro wat (spiced chicken stew) and misir wat (spiced lentil stew).

Ingredients

Adjust the serving size to calculate the ingredient quantities.

Servings: 4
  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 2.5 cups water, plus extra if needed
  • 0.5 teaspoon salt
  • 0.25 teaspoon dry yeast (optional)

Cooking Instructions

Step 1: Prepare the batter
  1. Place the teff flour in a large mixing bowl.
  2. Gradually add the water while whisking until you have a smooth batter.
  3. Add the optional yeast if you want to help start the fermentation process.
  4. Cover the bowl loosely with a clean cloth or lid that allows some airflow.
Step 2: Ferment the batter
  1. Leave the batter at room temperature for approximately 2 to 3 days.
  2. During fermentation, bubbles should develop and the batter should develop a pleasantly sour smell.
  3. A layer of liquid may form on top. Stir it back in or carefully pour off some of it before mixing.
  4. Do not use the batter if it develops fuzzy mould or an unpleasant rotten smell.
Step 3: Prepare the cooking batter
  1. Stir the fermented batter thoroughly.
  2. Add the salt and mix well.
  3. Add small amounts of water until the batter becomes thin and pourable, similar to crepe batter.
Step 4: Cook the injera
  1. Heat a non-stick pan or traditional injera cooking plate over medium heat.
  2. Lightly grease the pan if necessary.
  3. Pour the batter in a circular motion, starting from the outside and moving toward the centre.
  4. Cover the pan and cook for approximately 2 to 3 minutes.
  5. The surface should develop small holes and become set. Injera is traditionally not flipped.
  6. Carefully remove the flatbread and allow it to cool before stacking.
Step 5: Serve

Serve injera with Ethiopian or Eritrean dishes such as doro wat, misir wat, vegetable stews or other traditional sauces.

Where It Comes From

Injera is deeply connected to Ethiopian and Eritrean food culture. Addis Ababa, Ethiopia's capital, is an important centre of Ethiopian cuisine.
