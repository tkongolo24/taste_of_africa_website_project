Moroccan Tagine

Tagine is both the name of a traditional North African dish and the conical clay pot used to prepare it. Meat and vegetables are slowly cooked until tender, allowing the flavours to develop. Moroccan tagine recipes often feature preserved lemon, saffron and ras el hanout, reflecting the country's diverse culinary influences.

Ingredients

Adjust the serving size to calculate the ingredient quantities.

Servings: 4
  • 800 g lamb shoulder, cut into cubes
  • 2 onions, grated or finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 teaspoons ras el hanout
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 pinch saffron threads
  • 150 g dried prunes
  • 1 preserved lemon
  • 2 tablespoons toasted almonds
  • 250 ml water or stock
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt, or to taste

Cooking Instructions

Step 1: Marinate the lamb
  1. Place the lamb pieces in a large bowl.
  2. Add the minced garlic, ras el hanout, cinnamon, salt and olive oil.
  3. Mix thoroughly until the lamb is coated with the spices.
  4. Cover and allow the lamb to marinate for at least one hour.
Step 2: Prepare the tagine
  1. Heat the tagine or a heavy-bottomed pot over low to medium heat.
  2. Add the grated onions and cook until softened.
  3. Add the marinated lamb and brown lightly on all sides.
  4. Dissolve the saffron in warm water or stock, then pour it into the pot.
  5. Cover and cook gently for approximately 1.5 to 2 hours, until the lamb becomes tender. Check occasionally and add a little liquid if necessary.
Step 3: Add the prunes and preserved lemon
  1. Add the dried prunes to the pot.
  2. Rinse the preserved lemon, remove the pulp if desired, and add the peel to the dish.
  3. Continue cooking for approximately 20 minutes until the flavours combine.
Step 4: Garnish and serve
  1. Toast the almonds in a dry pan until golden.
  2. Sprinkle the almonds over the finished tagine.
  3. Serve hot with couscous, Moroccan bread or your preferred side dish.
