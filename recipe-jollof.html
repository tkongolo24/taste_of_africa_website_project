Jollof Rice

Jollof is a beloved one-pot rice dish across West Africa, with historical roots associated with the Wolof people of the Senegambia region. Nigeria and Ghana famously debate whose version is best. The dish is traditionally prepared with tomatoes, onions, and scotch bonnet peppers.

Ingredients

Adjust the serving size to calculate the ingredient quantities.

Servings: 4
  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 4 ripe tomatoes, blended
  • 2 red bell peppers, blended
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 3 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 3 cups chicken stock
  • 1 teaspoon thyme
  • 1 teaspoon curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt, or to taste

Cooking Instructions

Step 1: Prepare the pepper base
  1. Wash the tomatoes, bell peppers, scotch bonnet pepper and onions.
  2. Blend the tomatoes, bell peppers and scotch bonnet until smooth.
  3. Pour the mixture into a pot and simmer until it reduces and the raw smell disappears, approximately 15 minutes.
Step 2: Prepare the stew
  1. Heat cooking oil in a large pot.
  2. Add the sliced onions and fry until softened.
  3. Add the tomato paste and cook for a few minutes.
  4. Pour in the reduced pepper mixture and fry for approximately 10 minutes.
  5. Add thyme, curry powder and salt. Stir to combine.
Step 3: Cook the rice
  1. Rinse the rice thoroughly and drain the water.
  2. Add the rice to the stew and pour in the chicken stock.
  3. Stir gently until the rice is coated with the stew.
  4. Cover the pot tightly and cook over low heat for approximately 30 minutes, checking occasionally until the rice is tender.
  5. Allow the rice to rest for 10 minutes before fluffing with a fork and serving.
Step 4: Serve

Serve hot with fried plantain, grilled chicken, vegetables or your preferred side dish.

Where It Comes From

Jollof rice has historical connections to the Senegambia region of West Africa. Saint-Louis, Senegal, is one of the places associated with this history.
